# Noesis Evidence Review Protocol

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**Review type:** PRISMA 2020 systematic umbrella review with randomized-trial update  
**Status:** Pre-registered internally before search execution; not externally registered  
**Review owner:** Noesis project (AI-assisted; see Section 14)

---

## 1. Background and rationale

Noesis is intended to become a multimodal brain-training platform. "Brain training" is not one intervention. It can mean computerized cognitive training (CCT), cognitive remediation, adaptive games, EEG neurofeedback, audio-visual entrainment (AVE), exergames, or combinations with sleep, stress and biometric feedback. The evidence differs sharply by modality, population, comparator, dose and outcome. Marketing claims in this field have repeatedly outrun the evidence, and regulators have acted against unsupported cognitive-improvement claims.

A product built on a single optimistic paper would be indefensible. A product built on a narrow meta-analysis would also be indefensible, because transfer, blinding and population effects decide whether a training effect is meaningful. The review therefore asks not "does brain training work?" but "which modality produces which outcome, for whom, against which comparator, at what dose, with what certainty, and with what safety and regulatory constraints?"

The review is deliberately broad because the product is multimodal and intended for multiple populations. Breadth is managed by an umbrella design: systematic reviews and meta-analyses are the primary unit, with pivotal randomized controlled trials (RCTs) added where reviews are absent, stale, or insufficiently stratified.

### 1.1 Why an umbrella review

- The primary literature is too large for a single-trial review across all modalities and populations.
- High-quality meta-analyses already exist for CCT, neurofeedback, ADHD, older adults and some clinical populations.
- Overlap between reviews must be measured and handled; otherwise the same trials are counted repeatedly.
- The product needs effect-size ranges and certainty, not a list of every trial.

### 1.2 What this review will not do

- It will not establish clinical efficacy for any indication. That requires human systematic review, full-text appraisal, and probably prospective trials.
- It will not treat consumer-device marketing, app-store claims, or vendor white papers as evidence.
- It will not pool every modality into one "brain training" effect size.
- It will not make a medical claim or diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.

---

## 2. Objectives

**Primary objective:** Estimate the direction, magnitude and certainty of effects of multimodal brain-training interventions on cognitive, functional, clinical and safety outcomes across major population groups.

**Secondary objectives:**

1. Identify which design features (adaptivity, feedback, gamification, dose, social support, clinical supervision) are associated with adherence and efficacy.
2. Identify near-transfer and far-transfer boundaries, and the conditions under which far transfer has been demonstrated.
3. Identify safety, privacy and regulatory constraints for visual, auditory, EEG and biometric components.
4. Translate the evidence into product requirements, explicit non-claims, and a measurement plan for Noesis.
5. Produce a living evidence base that can be updated as the product evolves.

## 3. Review questions and PICO

### Q1. Computerized cognitive training and cognitive games

In children/adolescents, healthy adults, older adults (including MCI), and clinical populations, what is the effect of CCT, cognitive remediation, brain-training games or cognitively demanding exergames, compared with passive or active control, on near transfer, far transfer, everyday function, symptoms, and adverse events?

### Q2. Neurofeedback

In the same population strata, what is the effect of EEG neurofeedback (frequency-band, slow cortical potential, or connectivity training), including consumer EEG devices, compared with sham, active or usual-care control, on cognition, symptoms, everyday function, and adverse events?

### Q3. Audio-visual entrainment

In the same population strata, what is the effect of binaural beats, monaural beats, isochronic tones, photic stimulation, or combined audio-visual entrainment, compared with placebo/sham or no intervention, on cognition, mood, sleep, physiological arousal, and adverse events?

### Q4. Multimodal combinations

What is the effect of combining two or more modalities (for example CCT plus neurofeedback, CCT plus physical exercise, CCT plus AVE, or training plus sleep/stress/HRV feedback) compared with the components alone or a control condition?

### Q5. Design and delivery moderators

Which design and delivery features moderate adherence, engagement and efficacy: adaptive difficulty, feedback valence, game framing, session dose, schedule, coaching, social comparison, clinical supervision, or outcome feedback?

### Q6. Safety, privacy and regulation

What adverse events, contraindications, privacy risks and regulatory boundaries are documented for visual, auditory, EEG and biometric brain-training components?

### 3.1 PICO elements

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Population** | Humans of any age. Four pre-specified strata: (1) children and adolescents (under 18); (2) healthy adults (18-64); (3) older adults (65+) with normal cognition or mild cognitive impairment (MCI); (4) clinical populations (ADHD, schizophrenia/psychosis, stroke, traumatic brain injury, dementia, anxiety, depression, and other neurological or psychiatric conditions). |
| **Intervention** | CCT/cognitive games/cognitive remediation; EEG neurofeedback; AVE (binaural, monaural, isochronic, photic, combined); multimodal combinations. Non-invasive brain stimulation is included only when paired with a training intervention and analysed as a co-intervention, not as a stand-alone treatment. |
| **Comparator** | Passive control (waitlist, no contact, usual care) or active control (alternative computer activity, sham neurofeedback, placebo audio, low-dose training). Comparator type is extracted and analysed separately. |
| **Outcomes** | **Cognitive near transfer:** trained-task or same-domain test. **Cognitive far transfer:** untrained cognitive domain, academic achievement, or real-world performance. **Function:** everyday function, instrumental activities, work/school performance, quality of life. **Clinical:** symptoms, relapse, mood, sleep. **Process:** adherence, engagement, dropout. **Safety:** adverse events, photosensitivity, auditory discomfort, skin reactions, sleep disruption. |
| **Study designs** | Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of RCTs (primary); RCTs (update); controlled trials for safety only. Narrative reviews, commentaries, protocols, case reports, animal studies, and uncontrolled pre-post studies are excluded from efficacy synthesis. |
| **Time frame** | Published 2000-01-01 to 2026-10-31. Foundational trials published before 2000 are eligible when cited by an included review. |
| **Language** | English. This is a limitation, not a claim of completeness. |
| **Setting** | Any setting: home, school, clinic, laboratory, community. |

## 4. Eligibility criteria

### 4.1 Inclusion

1. Human participants.
2. One of the eligible interventions, alone or in a multimodal combination.
3. A comparator as defined above.
4. At least one eligible outcome.
5. Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs, or an RCT (for the update), or a controlled safety study.
6. Sufficient data to extract an effect estimate or enough information to describe the result.
7. Peer-reviewed publication; preprints only for emerging technology/safety questions and labelled as preprint.

### 4.2 Exclusion

1. Animal or in-vitro studies.
2. No cognitive, functional, clinical or safety outcome.
3. Uncontrolled studies, case series, case reports, opinions, editorials, conference abstracts without extractable data.
4. Study protocols and registered trials without results (these are logged separately as ongoing).
5. Interventions that are primarily physical training without a cognitive demand, unless combined with a cognitive component and analysed as multimodal.
6. Stand-alone pharmacological, electrical-stimulation, or surgical interventions.
7. Commercial or vendor material presented as evidence.
8. Duplicate publications of the same dataset: the most complete report is retained; the duplicate is linked.

### 4.3 Prioritization when reviews overlap

When multiple reviews cover the same trials and outcome, the review with the most comprehensive search, most recent search date, pre-specified methods, and lowest AMSTAR 2 concerns is used for the primary estimate. Overlap is quantified with the corrected covered area (CCA). Reviews with high overlap are retained for sensitivity analysis, not double-counted.

## 6. Search strategy

The full strings, dates, limits and result counts are recorded in `SEARCH_STRATEGY.md` and `SEARCH_LOG.md`. The strategy combines:

1. **Modality block:** "computerized cognitive training", "cognitive training", "cognitive remediation", "brain training", "brain-training game", "cognitive game", "exergame", "neurofeedback", "EEG biofeedback", "binaural beat", "monaural beat", "isochronic tone", "audio-visual entrainment", "photic stimulation".
2. **Population block:** child, adolescent, adult, older adult, aged, mild cognitive impairment, ADHD, schizophrenia, psychosis, stroke, traumatic brain injury, dementia, anxiety, depression.
3. **Outcome block:** cognition, cognitive function, memory, working memory, attention, executive function, processing speed, intelligence, academic achievement, daily function, quality of life, symptoms, adverse events.
4. **Design block:** systematic review, meta-analysis, randomized controlled trial, controlled trial.

The broad search is run without a population filter to avoid missing trials that do not use population labels in the abstract. Population is applied at screening and extraction.

### 6.1 Search validation

- A known-item set of 20 landmark papers is used to check recall (for example the ACTIVE trial, the FINGER trial, key working-memory meta-analyses, the ADHD neurofeedback meta-analyses, and the binaural-beat meta-analysis).
- If a known item is not retrieved, the string is revised and the revision is logged.
- Search results are deduplicated by DOI, PMID and normalized title.

### 6.2 Search dates

- Bibliographic searches: 2026-10-31.
- Citation chasing and regulatory checks: 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-02.
- Update searches: planned quarterly after the first release.

## 7. Selection process

1. **Title/abstract screening:** AI-assisted first pass against the inclusion/exclusion criteria.
2. **Second-pass verification:** a random 20% sample plus all uncertain records are re-screened independently with the same criteria; disagreements are resolved by discussion against the protocol. Agreement is reported (percentage and Cohen's kappa).
3. **Full-text screening:** all potentially eligible records are assessed. Reasons for exclusion are recorded in the PRISMA flow.
4. **Citation chasing:** backward and forward citations of included reviews and pivotal trials.
5. **Ongoing trials:** registered trials without results are logged but not included in efficacy synthesis.

Because the first pass is AI-assisted, the review is labelled **AI-assisted** throughout. It is decision support for product design, not a substitute for a human-led clinical systematic review.

---

## 8. Data collection and extraction

A piloted extraction form is used. The following fields are collected:

- Record identifiers: PMID, DOI, OpenAlex ID, title, authors, year, journal, publication type.
- Review/trial design and registration.
- Population: age range, mean age, sex, diagnosis, baseline severity, setting.
- Intervention: modality, device/software, task(s), adaptive algorithm, dose (sessions, minutes, weeks), supervision, co-interventions.
- Comparator: passive, active, sham, usual care; blinding of participants, deliverers, outcome assessors.
- Outcomes: instrument, domain, near/far transfer, time point, effect measure, effect size with 95% CI, mean difference, responder rate.
- Safety: adverse events, dropouts due to adverse events, contraindications.
- Adherence: completion rate, sessions attended, engagement metrics.
- Funding and conflicts of interest.
- Risk of bias and certainty ratings.
- Notes on overlap with other reviews.

Double extraction is performed on a 20% random sample. Discrepancies are resolved by re-reading the source and recording the resolution.

## 9. Risk of bias and certainty

| Unit | Tool | Domains |
|---|---|---|
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | AMSTAR 2 | Protocol, search, selection, extraction, RoB, synthesis, publication bias, COI |
| RCT | RoB 2 | Randomization, deviations, missing data, outcome measurement, selective reporting |
| Certainty of evidence | GRADE | Risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias |

For reviews, overlap is assessed with CCA. For meta-analyses, the presence of a pre-registered protocol, a comprehensive search, and an assessment of publication bias is recorded. Industry-funded studies are flagged, not automatically excluded.

## 10. Effect measures and synthesis

### 10.1 Effect measures

- Continuous outcomes: standardized mean difference (Hedges' g) with 95% CI when instruments differ; mean difference when the same instrument is used.
- Binary outcomes: risk ratio or odds ratio with 95% CI.
- Time-to-event: hazard ratio where available.
- Interpretation: 0.2 = small, 0.5 = moderate, 0.8 = large, with the caveat that a statistically significant small effect may be clinically trivial.

### 10.2 Synthesis rules

1. **Do not pool across modalities.** CCT, neurofeedback, AVE and multimodal interventions are separate syntheses.
2. **Do not pool across population strata** unless a formal subgroup analysis is pre-specified and powered.
3. **Separate near transfer from far transfer.** A trained-task gain is not evidence of general cognitive improvement.
4. **Separate active from passive comparators.** Effects against waitlist are not equivalent to effects against an active control.
5. **Separate blinded from unblinded outcome assessment.**
6. **Random-effects meta-analysis** when at least three homogeneous studies/reviews report the same outcome; otherwise narrative synthesis with a structured summary.
7. **Overlap handling:** when reviews overlap, the primary estimate comes from the pre-specified prioritized review; sensitivity analyses use alternative reviews.
8. **Durability:** immediate post-test and follow-up (≤3 months, 3-12 months, >12 months) are reported separately.

### 10.3 Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

- Population stratum (children, healthy adults, older adults/MCI, clinical).
- Comparator (passive vs active/sham).
- Dose (total training hours; low/medium/high).
- Adaptivity (adaptive vs fixed difficulty).
- Supervision (self-administered vs coached/clinician-delivered).
- Blinding (blinded vs unblinded outcome assessment).
- Baseline cognition (normal vs impaired).
- Industry funding.
- Risk of bias (low/some concerns vs high).
- Small-study effects and publication bias where at least 10 studies are available.

### 10.4 Meta-analysis

If the included reviews provide sufficient homogeneous trial-level data, a random-effects meta-analysis will be conducted with `metafor`/R or Python. If only review-level estimates are available, a narrative synthesis with harvest plots and effect-direction tables will be used. The analysis code and extracted data will be archived with the review.

---

## 11. Reporting bias

- Published protocols and trial registrations are compared with published results where possible.
- Funnel plots, Egger's test and trim-and-fill are used only when at least 10 independent estimates are available.
- Selective outcome reporting is assessed in RoB 2 and AMSTAR 2.
- Industry-funded reviews and trials are flagged.
- Unpublished trials in ClinicalTrials.gov are logged; missing results are reported as a gap.

---

## 12. Certainty and interpretation

GRADE certainty is rated for each modality × population × outcome comparison:

- **High:** further research is very unlikely to change confidence.
- **Moderate:** further research is likely to have an important impact.
- **Low:** further research is very likely to have an important impact.
- **Very low:** any estimate is very uncertain.

The review will explicitly separate:

- **Evidence of near transfer** (trained task/domain).
- **Evidence of far transfer** (untrained domain, academic or everyday function).
- **Evidence of clinical benefit** (symptoms, function, quality of life).
- **Evidence of safety** (adverse events and contraindications).
- **Evidence of engagement** (adherence, dropout, sustained use).

A product requirement is only labelled "evidence-supported" when the relevant modality × population × outcome comparison reaches at least **low certainty** and the effect is not explained by passive-control or unblinded assessment alone. Otherwise it is labelled "experimental" or "wellness-only."

---

## 13. Limitations

1. English-language restriction.
2. No direct access to PsycINFO, Embase, CINAHL or ERIC.
3. AI-assisted screening and extraction; human verification is required before clinical use.
4. Review-level evidence inherits the flaws of the included reviews and trials.
5. Overlap between reviews can inflate apparent consistency; CCA and prioritization rules mitigate but do not eliminate this.
6. Consumer devices and apps often lack peer-reviewed evidence; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, but it is a product risk.
7. The field changes quickly; the review is a snapshot with a planned update cycle.
8. "All populations" cannot be equally powered; some strata will have sparse evidence.

---

## 14. AI-assistance disclosure

This review is conducted by an AI agent under human direction. The AI:

- generates and executes reproducible searches;
- screens titles/abstracts and extracts data;
- drafts the synthesis and certainty judgements;
- flags uncertainty and missing evidence.

The AI does **not**:

- claim to be a human reviewer;
- provide medical advice;
- substitute for a human systematic review before clinical claims;
- invent citations, effect sizes or regulatory decisions.

Every quantitative claim in the final review must be traceable to a retrieved record or full text. If a source cannot be verified, the claim is marked "unverified" or removed.
